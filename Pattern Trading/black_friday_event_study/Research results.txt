FIRST RESEARCH RESULT - 

This is much healthier-looking than the logistics pre-event result because:

The means and medians agree.
Most years are positive.
The effect appears in two economically related groups.
Both groups subsequently have negative Late_post returns.

For retail:

Immediate event: +1.42%
Late post: −0.84%

For logistics:

Immediate event: +0.81%
Late post: −0.62%

That is directionally consistent with:

Stocks exposed to Black Friday rise around Black Friday week and subsequently give back part of the gain.

This is currently the most promising pattern in the project.

But it is still exploratory

The raw p-values are:

Retail immediate event: approximately 0.092
Logistics immediate event: approximately 0.087

Neither is conventionally significant, and both become approximately 0.46 after FDR correction.

Retail being positive in 12 of 16 years is notable. A simple one-sided sign test gives approximately p = 0.038, but we cannot treat that as confirmed evidence because we discovered it after examining several groups and windows.

So this becomes a new hypothesis to validate, not a finished result.



***FURTHERMORE ****

- Revised main hypothesis

US retail and e-commerce stocks generate positive abnormal returns during the five trading days preceding Black Friday through the following Monday.

This is now the project’s strongest candidate.



- The retail immediate-event result is strong enough to continue investigating, but not yet strong enough to call an investment strategy. The next two gates are company-level breadth and placebo-window testing.


- Certain highly Black-Friday-sensitive retailers—particularly Macy’s and possibly Etsy—have historically generated positive abnormal returns around Black Friday week.
